The Solar Century

Earth is lost. Build the Moonbase, explore the real Solar System, and launch the Gaia Ark before 2200.
Genre Colony management and strategyPlatform Browser, single fileStatus Early prototypeInspired by Millennium 2.2 (1989)

About this document

This is the Game Design Document (GDD) for The Solar Century. It describes what the game is, how its systems work and fit together, and what is still to be designed. It is a living document: it is updated together with the game whenever a feature is added or changed, and its revision number, date and history (below, last section) are filled in from git by tools/sync-gdd.js.

  • Built: in the prototype today. Partial: started, with parts missing. Planned: promised but not started.
  • All numbers are provisional. Balancing is deliberately postponed. The live values are the Default configuration in the game's Editor; the tables here mirror them at the revision shown.
  • Companion files: README.md (what the prototype contains), PROMISES.md (what we said we will do later) and CLAUDE.md (technical guide and working rules).

Vision

Premise

In the year 2100 an asteroid has scorched the Earth. The last of humanity lives in a small base in Mare Tranquillitatis, on the Moon. The colony has one century to grow from a handful of domes into a spacefaring civilisation, reach out across the Solar System for the resources it lacks, and build the Gaia Ark: a ship that carries the seeds of new life back to Earth.

Pillars

A colony to keep alive

Every colonist matters. They eat water and oxygen, take jobs in buildings, crew ships and are born or die. Running short of air or water costs lives.

An economy to build

Nineteen resources in five families flow through mines, extractors, factories and reactors. Power, staffing and machinery wear decide how well it runs.

The real Solar System

A real calendar and real orbits: planets sit where they really are on the game date, so trips and launch windows change with time. Distance is the main obstacle.

A race against time

The deadline is 1 January 2200. Research better drives, survey and colonise new worlds, and gather the rare materials the Ark needs before the century ends.

Win and loss

  • Victory: research the Gaia seed, gather its materials and launch the Gaia Ark mission. When it reaches Earth, the game is won.
  • Defeat: the population falls to zero, or the year 2200 arrives before the Ark has launched.

Tone and look

Hard science fiction told with restraint, in the spirit of late-1980s strategy games: an Amiga/Atari ST interface of bevelled panels and orange accents, terse log messages, and a quiet main screen that shows the place where the player is.

Core loop

The player staffs buildings, which turn power and raw materials into resources and research points. Resources pay for more buildings, starships and missions; research unlocks better buildings, drives and colonies. Each colony opens new resources, which feed the next step outwards, until the Ark can be built.

Progression arc

Early game. Balance water, oxygen and power on the Moon. Build mines, an ice extractor and a Science Lab. Salvage Earth for metals.
Mid game. Research drives, survey the inner planets and the belt, found the first colonies, and run expeditions for copper, platinum and water.
Late game. Reach the outer moons for silver, chromium and uranium, research the Gaia seed, and assemble the Ark before 2200.
The arc above is the intended shape. Today only the Moonbase exists and produces; the mid and late game rely on missions that return fixed rewards.

Time and calendar

  • One tick is one game hour. The game starts on Friday 1 January and ends on 1 January (the deadline).
  • The calendar is the real Gregorian one: real month lengths, leap years (2100 is not one) and weekdays.
  • Rates and durations in the data use 720 hours as one game month. Daily checks (deaths from lack of air or water) run at midnight; monthly events (births, random events) on the first of the month.
  • The clock has pause, +1 hour and five speeds, from 1 hour per 30 seconds to 1 hour per half second.
  • Real orbits use the days since J2000, so every date in the century shows the planets in their true positions.

Colonists and jobs

The colony starts with colonists and Habitat modules ( colonists each). Each colonist uses water, food and the gas its lifeform breathes (Humans and Martians oxygen, Hydrogoids hydrogen, Nitroids nitrogen, Sulphuroids sulphur, Methanoids methane); the rates per lifeform are in the table below. Food comes from the Hydroponics building and water from the Ice extractor. The Water recycling technology cuts water use by 40%. Without water, food or the breathable gas, one colonist dies each day.

  • Births: each month one child is born if there is room in the habitats and the stock of water, food and breathable gas covers at least 3 months of the colony's needs.
  • Deaths: each day with no water, no food or no breathable gas, one colonist dies. Ships lost on missions lose their crew.
  • Jobs: free colonists are assigned to buildings as Labourers, Scientists, Technicians or Builders, or leave as Crew on a starship. A building needs a minimum staff to run and gets a +% boost when fully manned.
  • If the population falls below the posts filled, the largest teams lose people first and the log reports the empty jobs.

Kinds of colonist (design decided, not built yet)

  • Lifeforms: a colonist is Human, Martian, Hydrogoid, Nitroid, Sulphuroid or Methanoid. Each lifeform is based on the atmospheric conditions of a location. Lifeform per location: Human (Moon, Callisto, Enceladus, Tethys, Rhea, Miranda, Ariel, Oberon, Leda, Phoebe), Martian (Mars), Hydrogoid (Saturn), Nitroid (Titan), Sulphuroid (Umbriel, Hyperion), Methanoid (Uranus, Titania, Neptune, Triton, Pluto, Charon). Venus and Io are also Sulphuroid, Jupiter Hydrogoid, Phobos and Deimos Martian, and Europa, Ganymede, Dione, Iapetus and Mimas Human. Mercury, Earth and the asteroid belt have no lifeform and cannot host a colony. Resources per location come from the owner's table; the ones the table lacks were extrapolated from similar locations.
  • Base job: every colonist, of any lifeform, has "Colonist" as their base job.
  • Jobs: any colonist can work as a Labourer, Scientist, Technician, Builder or Crew.
Not built yet: colonists do not have a lifeform yet (only locations do); how a lifeform relates to atmosphere and to environmental technology). Colonists, Labourers, Scientists, Technicians, Builders and Crew have no Database descriptions yet.

Resources

Nineteen resources in five families, always shown in the same order. The Resources bar shows each quantity (k and M above 999), green when growing, normal when stale and red when falling. Every unit has a weight in tonnes, which matters for starship loading.

Research points

A separate currency earned by staffed Science Labs and some missions, spent on technologies (see Research).

Software has no source yet, and methane has no use (as in the original). Database descriptions are still to be written for all 19 resources.

Buildings and power

Buildings are ordered in Construction, paid up front, and finished after their build time by Builders. Each building on the queue needs at least 2 builders to progress and gets the boost with 4. A stalled site is reported in the log.

Power

Solar arrays and the Fission reactor produce power; every other building uses it. When demand exceeds supply, all machines run at the ratio of supply to demand (a brown-out), slowing production and research. Fusion cells raise solar output by 50%. A reactor without uranium or machinery produces nothing.

Machinery wear

Industrial buildings use up % of the machinery in their build cost each month. Without machinery they stop.

Research

  • Staffed Science Labs produce 1 research point per 20 days each. While a project is running, passive research drops to 25%.
  • Starting a project costs its full price up front, then 1% of it per day until done. A staffed lab is needed to start or keep a project. Running out of points or scientists pauses it; resuming costs half the price.
  • Only one project runs at a time at the Moonbase. Several outposts researching in parallel are planned.
  • Every technology belongs to one of seven categories, which are the filters of the Technology overview.

Generated technologies

Each successful survey creates a technology Colonise <location> (Colonization category). Its cost grows with the location's analysis difficulty and length: × (1 + × difficulty) × (1 + length / ) points.

No technologies belong to Manufacturing or Weapons yet. Drive technologies (faster than the simplest drive), environmental technologies per atmosphere type, and a technology per starship part are still to be defined.

Starport and starships

Starport

The Starport is a Moonbase screen, not a building. It starts with berth and grows to , one berth at a time through the Construction queue (, days each). Each ship needs a berth; a ship away on a mission keeps its berth.

Starship types

A probe costs in the Shipyard and is berthed automatically. Recycling a landed ship returns % of its cost and whatever it carries.

Parts

Every starship has eight part slots, each to be tied to a technology. Parts have a weight; the ship's tonnage is its parts plus its crew and cargo, against a maximum ( t for probes, placeholder).

Loading

Each ship in the Starships overview has four tabs. Crew takes free colonists aboard by kind (Colonists, Labourers, Scientists, Technicians, Builders, Crew). Resources loads units from the base stock. Both work only on a landed ship and stop at the maximum tonnage. Cargo will hold parts and colony modules. Parts shows the fitted parts.

Still to define: what each part does per ship type, Refit (cost, time, which parts), the minimum crew and its warning, crew quarters limiting people aboard, cargo bay capacity, how weight affects the drive, and how missions use what a ship carries.

Missions

A mission takes a berthed ship and a crew of free colonists, pays its fuel up front and returns after its duration with fixed rewards, unless the ship is lost (its risk), taking the crew with it. The Gaia Ark launch wins the game.

These missions predate the survey and colonisation system and use fixed durations. They are expected to give way to real trips and colonies.

Trip time

The probe leaves on day t0 and flies straight at days per AU (the simplest drive: Moon to Earth in about days) towards where the target will be. The trip is the first time T at which speed × T reaches the distance to the target's real position at t0 + T. Inside one planetary system the orbit distance is used; moons share their planet's position; the belt uses Ceres. Trips therefore change with the date, which creates launch windows. A recall computes the way back from the current date.

Analysis

Success chance is 1 − difficulty × . The difficulty (0 to 1) and length in days are set per location in the Editor (see Locations).

Still to define: what a finished module does (launching it and founding the colony), what an analysis reveals, the colony module's size and contents beyond its cost, how colonists and supplies travel aboard the ship, and the real values for difficulty, length and cost. With the simplest drive nothing beyond Uranus is reachable before 2200, so faster drives are needed.

Locations

34 locations: the Sun's planets, Pluto, the asteroid belt (as one location) and 23 moons, in three regions. Every location has a placeholder scene, a description, what it can provide, an analysis difficulty and length, and its real orbit (distance, eccentricity, period). Only the Moonbase exists and produces.

The map

The main screen is a seven-level zoom of the Solar System, drawn behind the floating UI. Levels 1 to 4 are to scale around the Sun, with real orbit ellipses and planets in their real positions for the game date. The plane is tilted 40° towards the viewer; nearer bodies are drawn in front and bodies hidden behind them become see-through ghosts.

Interface and presentation

Rendering rules

  • The aspect ratio is 16:9 and the reference resolution is 1920×1080. The game is designed at that size and scaled to fit the window.
  • The main screen is a view of the place where the player is (the Moonbase today), filling the whole stage at the back.
  • All UI floats in front of it: the Resources and Research points bars, the overview screens, the date, the clock and the main menu.

Style

  • Amiga/Atari ST look: bevelled buttons and panels, deep navy, orange accent.
  • One font, Jura (SIL Open Font License), with tightened letter-spacing.
  • Status shown with icons; details in hover tooltips. Greyed-out items stay readable.
  • Small 16×16 drawn icons for every resource, category and part.
  • Every overview screen has a 75px title bar with a centred title and a red-tinted close button. Filters and tabs stay pinned while the content scrolls.
  • The colony name sits alone in the centre at the top, in orange.
  • The date is large and bold, with weekday and time stacked at its right.
  • Dark and light themes, switched from the main menu button (hamburger) and remembered; this document follows the same choice.
